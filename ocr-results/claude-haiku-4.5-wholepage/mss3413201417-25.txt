ELECTION IN PENNSYLVANIA, INDIANA AND OHIO.

[Multiple newspaper articles discussing elections in these states]

The following dispatch has been transmitted from Indianapolis:—

INDIANAPOLIS, Oct. 10.—A meeting was held to-night at Turner Hall, at which speeches were made by Hon. A. P. Stone, chairman of the Democratic State Committee, and other Democratic speakers, such, at the same time, however, by direct request of the Committee and the national Democratic Executive Committee, that no reference to a single candidate, either local or national, was made. It is impossible to tell government to them who refused to light of their objections to the Republican party. As a people we have been denied the unerrer choice in our political faith, and it is right that the people should make a choice as to their governors and rulers. But what does it matter whether they are depict and darker than the path over which we have come? A voice came from the crowd: "We will never have the daughters violated, our fires kindled, our business destroyed; one voice cried out that his house would be burned, and will the honor of the State be lost? As to the main issue to come, we gladly went to fight for justice, but were denied and put off by the power itself to fight. We knew then that in a day the Union itself would have been denied the bonds of slavery. We have fought a long fight, where victory gave us right, and now we want the same for our State in the election to come."

It was announced that Prof Godwin Smith would take the chair in the course of the evening. The result of the vote on the question of war and peace issues taken up, but the later on the 21st of November. Next Tuesday's vote will show to the Tribune in Pittsburg whether the State will go right.
