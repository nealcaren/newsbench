## Industrial Equality

### Ella M. Sherwin Speaks Before League of Women Voters

Monday, March 12, the League of Women Voters of Pennsylvania held its annual convention at Hotel Pennsylvania. Mrs. Ada R. Wolf had been asked to speak on Industrial Equality, but in the case of a previous engagement, and Miss Ella M. Sherwin, corresponding secretary of the New York State League, took up the verbal cudgels in our first stand against so-called protective legislation.

Mrs. Wolf, president of the League of Women Voters of Brooklyn and her uncompromising attitude against short and long hours, was particularly concerned in by many other women who are members of the Women Voters League. In this connection Miss Sherwin’s speech was listened to with much interest by her audience, and after the meeting, many women came to her to express their appreciation.

Mrs. Frances Perkins, Industrial Commissioner, spoke in the affirmative, but, as at the hearing in Albany, she was not able to make the women feel that the “flapper” in industry—and we have always contended that the he-flapper and the she-flapper should be treated alike—is not a subject for legislation, from leaving school too soon and from entering industry in competition with men. The eight-hour law for minors of both sexes up to 18 would be a proper exercise of the State’s authority over the young, but to extend this to adult women would discourage their employment.

### Rhode Island Oppose 48-Hour BILLS

Hearings on the Rhode Island eight-hour bills (there are seven of them, one including the prohibition of night shift work after 6 p. m. and before 6 a. m.) were held before the Labor Committee of the General Assembly. Mrs. Mary A. Murray, president of the Rhode Island Woman’s Equal Opportunity League, who has been a night shift worker and against the eight-hour bills, said:

“All working at night, the exclusion of women from the night shift work after 6 p. m. and before 6 a. m. is an unwarranted assumption of the police power of the State.”

Mrs. Murray, who is a member of the National Woman’s Party, said that the bill is an unwarranted assumption of the police power of the State and that it is an unwarranted assumption of the police power of the State.
