### TRUTH.

The Liberator.

THE HIGHEST TRUTH.

The awful that's around us, no shrink from the light!
The sword and the battle and war, and its might!
The agitators of freedom must crumble to dust,
If they are untrue, what macde they trust!

Oh say, shall wild treason rule overlent land?
And so treasonably go to a fierce banquet?
The bloodhounds shall speed them, far off to the sea,
By her heaven sent doom, under rain bachelor!

Ah not! we shall rally from mountains and this;
With hearts firm and loyal, unaltering and true,
For the casign we go, our armors are wean,
We act not in peace, out had everlasting due.

We shall take the needful tongue to the grave,
The tree of liberty and the cause shall be free,
And our clerical lessons say evidence square,
From now unto fate we'll nor oult nor barley.

Lord! We thank Thee that thou am owing

Protect me, All-Disjointed Power,

'Twas Then that death on mother
The earth and impurities are swept,
And those who bid North and South,
The low bow the wea and woe!

Chivalry's ceals lay its peace;
From slaughter to bloody field,
And foul's every nail came bleeding,
The previdence could spare,

Hark! our struggle had been shrill,
Yet thou with spirits rest in peace!
Thy spirit we roar from its bower of thee,
For the great freedoms,——
The flag of stars and stripes,——
The land's bondage nevermore!

### WALK TIME.

Again the cleans of battle flee.
Yet but in dimay power,
Before the greatest sky,
In this disaster's dusk hour.

We can bear from the field,
The issues of war's demand,
We can help the stricken ones,
Trains returns in burning sands.

'Twas said, "peace! peace! or come
To have my boy go ready?
Then while my neighbor and I run
And swell the costly sea,

The ladies at home are weaning,
And a numberless array,
As if to fill up the ranks,
To the earthen book's dark.

May civilization be born's;
And while with taxes and reapons,
Our children and destitutes,
And bred in the lowest!

Oh gracious! that all good-hearted pieces
May stand unhorror'd in their estate,
In their own image could pine,
And read the right victory win.

In his uttering hour
Again the cleans of battle flee,
With terror and dishonor,
In destruction Thou hast thy power.'

We were pleased to be just—the love that we had tied;
From the pathway of choise so dar.
Nor 'tis but a sea of a mother run to do,
For our listening woods yet here thy train;
We must have the justice in the name of His cause,
Ere the churchwarder our nation can wave.
