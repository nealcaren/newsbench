The next day Washington, leaving Newport, made a visit to Wickford, which was then a place of considerable importance. His route was through East Greenwich and Wickford Landing. He set out from Newport in a carriage drawn by four horses. He was escorted by the whole of Col Barton’s troops, whose commander was at his side. At Wickford, he dined with the officers of the garrison at the house of a gentleman named Anthony. After the dinner, he reviewed his troops. The next day he made an excursion to his headquarters, where he dined with Col. Barton. He lodged that night at the home of Colonel Barton, and the next day he returned to Newport, where he spent the evening of his visit to Aquidneck.

At Newport, Washington felt called upon to make special acknowledgements of the hospitable treatment he had received during his visit. In his official report, he referred to the entertainment as “hospitable to a fault.” He departed Wednesday evening, April 10, 1776, for New London.
