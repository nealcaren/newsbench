Members of the new Woman Suffrage party of Providence in Ward 1, District 1, held their first regular meeting last evening at the home of Mrs. Elizabeth Monks, 159 Benefit street. A definite plan was adopted for a regular canvass of the district and the spreading of woman suffrage propaganda.

The first thing that Washington did after the dying out of the excitement attendant upon his arrival was to visit the college grounds in company with Dr. Manning. In the evening he also went to the campus to view the illuminations. Next morning he showed his interest in the town's principal industry by going down to one of the shipyards and viewing one of Brown & Ives's big Indiamen then in the stocks.

In the afternoon he and his party were guests at an entertainment and reception held in the Court House. Again 13 toasts were drunk and again the President remarked upon the hospitality of his friends in Providence. "It may be proper to remark," added the Gazette, "that no untoward accident took place, that every countenance indicated the most heartfelt joy and that we have reason to believe the President was perfectly satisfied with his reception."

The following morning Washington set sail in the Hancock for New York, never again to honor either Providence or Rhode Island by a visit.
