THE PROVIDENCE JOURNAL, SATURDAY, APRIL 19, 1913

# SUFFRAGISTS PLAN BIG MEETING.

## Rhode Island Organization Holds Its First Session.

The Rhode Island Woman Suffrage Party yesterday afternoon held its first session since Organization, April 11, and laid plans for a large meeting to be held in Manning Hall, Brown University, May 2. Prof. J. C. Dunning of the Brown faculty is expected to be the speaker.

Yesterday’s meeting was presided over by General Chairman Mrs. James W. Algeo, who, before entering upon the definite business announced, explained in detail some negotiations with proposed speakers for future meetings and asked the opinion of members respecting the proposed subject, “Political Machinery of Rhode Island,” with the result that it was decided to secure, if possible, Prof. J. C. Dunning to speak upon this subject at a meeting to be held at Manning Hall, Friday evening, May 2. Mrs. Algeo referred to finding a growing interest in the different phases of the suffrage movement, a desire to gain a better understanding of what it meant, and this desire she explained to the members present was their opportunity.

Reports were then presented by the Secretary, Mrs. Sarah L. Fitz, and the Treasurer, Mrs. George W. Parks, after which Mrs. Algeo outlined what she had done by way of propaganda during the week. This included addressing a meeting in the Pawtuxet Valley, where plans were formulated for an outdoor meeting to be held in June, arranging to speak at Pawtucket next Monday and attending a meeting of colored suffragists last Friday evening who, she reported, were greatly interested, but needed information which she tried to give in a brief address.

This, with the exception of what was styled a “tentative appointment” to speak at Woonsocket in the near future, completed the recital.

Miss H. A. Thomas, the leader in the first district, Ward 1, gave a report of the meetings which she had been instrumental in holding each month since December last, and referred to two meetings held by her among the colored suffragists, concluding with mention of a suffrage social to be held at 19 Congdon street this evening. In connection with the work among the colored women, Miss Thomas told of special interest aroused by an address made by Mrs. Maud Howe.

FRIDAY, APRIL 19, 1913.

LOCAL SUFFRAGISTS  
TO TAKE UP THE FIGHT

# Mrs. Algeo Outlines Plans For Furthering Movement in this City.

A plan to swing Pawtucket into the onward march of equal suffrage was outlined by Mrs. James W. Algeo of Providence to a gathering of Pawtucket women in the parlor of Miss Althea L. Hall’s home at 188 High street, last night. If this plan is followed out the equal suffrage adherents in this city will have six ward leaders, three captains in each ward, monthly meetings in various wards, meetings of leaders to shape the general plan of campaign, and public meetings, (indoors or out) as the occasion may require, with prominent speakers to help boost the cause.

The leaders and captains will be furnished with guide books giving rules for successful organization. Step by step, these books will point the way to a machine as strong and powerful by comparison as the Tammany organization of New York or the anti-Democratic Society of this city.

Woe to the candidate for office who openly opposes equal suffrage in these parts. Working along strictly political lines and using well-defined political methods invented by men, the local suffragists will set him up as a target and bring all possible pressure to bear against his candidate.

# Free Lectures at Brown.

Claude C. Ball will speak at Manning Hall, Brown University. Friday evening at 8 o'clock on “The Election Machinery of Rhode Island.” On the following evening Prof. Dunning will talk on “Political Machinery of Rhode Island.” These lectures, which are to be free, will be given under the auspices of the Woman Suffrage party.

# Will Hold Suffrage Meeting.

Mrs. Jerome M. Fittz will address the Suffrage Party of District 1, Ward 1, at the home of the district leader, Miss Helen A. Thomas, 54 Meeting street, this evening. The speaker, who formerly lived in New York city, will tell of the work of the suffragists there. Tea will be served after the meeting.
