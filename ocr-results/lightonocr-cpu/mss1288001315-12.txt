He dealt, with two or three of these items, including the alterations to premises, and additions, &c. There was in all companies he belonged to a depreciation fund, and they had nothing like a reserve for depreciation which such a company should have. It did not appear to him wise to pay a dividend of 6 per cent., though it only made an average of $4\frac{1}{2}$ . (The CHAIRMAN: Why don't you take the average from the beginning; you know the reason why $2\frac{1}{2}$ was paid.) Well, the rule all companies adopted was to take an average of the three years last passed, and on that ground he spoke. If they took the previous years, and divided them, it would not come to very much more. He urged the reduction of outstanding liabilities, and said that nearly every item of expenditure had been reduced, the exception being the renewal of furniture, and he had no doubt that the money was properly laid out.
