**Poetry.**

For The Liberator.

THE NEGRO.

Man of the South, we bid you look to light!
Your sons are call'd to battle in the fight.
Your Union seeks the brave to stand and fall,
But dares not trust the Negro, one and all!

Oh, why this haste to arm another’s son?
And leave your own, whose backs have seldom run?
You chant of Freedom, Patriotism’s strain,
And ask the Negro still to bear the chain!

Proclaim your Rights, and let the Echo ring—
“All men are born free and equal thing!”
But this one truth, you blotted from your creed,
For wealth and power and self-seeking greed.

Yet still we hope, when darkness veils the land,
The light of Justice, guided by God’s hand,
Shall pierce the gloom, and break the bondman’s chain,
And Freedom’s trumpet sound through all the plain!

W.H.C.

**TESTIMONY OF COLORED WITNESSES.** 15

A Correspondent of the New York Tribune gives a most affecting and impartial account of the treatment of colored men taken prisoner at Fort Pillow. He states, among other things, that the officer commanding the rebel troops – the one who testified before a committee of Congress – was known as one of the most moderate and prudent officers in the Confederate army. The following are extracts from his account:—

“The conduct of our men was as disgraceful as any thing of which I have ever heard. The rebel troops, as soon as they reached the fort, commenced a massacre, shooting down the Negro soldiers as they surrendered. I saw one rebellious soldier deliberately shoot a colored man who was kneeling with his hands up, begging for his life. It was a sight I shall never forget. The rebel officers made no attempt to stop the murder, and some of them were actually seen to encourage it. The only thing that saved more of our men from being killed was the fact that the rebels had expended nearly all their ammunition.”

There are other witnesses, whose depositions confirm this statement. The complicity of the rebel authorities in this brutal murder is fully established. They have given no explanation, made no apology, and offered no excuse. This is but one instance of the manner in which the rebel authorities treat the colored soldiers and all who sympathize with the Union cause.

**The President’s Message.**

We publish in another column the President’s Message, sent to Congress on the 6th inst. It is a great document!—full of solemn reflections, and weighty considerations. It displays his usual strong sense, his admirable judgment, and his earnest devotion to the cause of the Union. We commend it to the careful perusal of our readers.

**Massachusetts Colored Regiment.**

The 54th Massachusetts Regiment, with its gallant officers and men, is enjoying a well-deserved respite near Hilton Head. They have recently participated in two battles—at James’ Island and Secessionville—and have acquitted themselves with great credit. The officers and men are in fine spirits, and eager for another fight. The regiment is a source of pride to the Commonwealth, and its valor has won the admiration of all who have witnessed its deeds.

**A Colored Preacher’s Appeal.**

Rev. Wm. J. Grimes, a colored preacher of New Haven, Conn., has issued an earnest and patriotic appeal to the colored people of the State, calling upon them to enlist in the Union army.

**WHAT GEN. BURNSIDE SAID.**

The N.Y. Tribune publishes a letter from a friend at Knoxville, stating that Gen. Burnside, the other day, in addressing his soldiers, said:—

“I have always been a Democrat. I was raised a Democrat, and have always voted the Democratic ticket. But I tell you, this is not a question of Democracy or Republicanism—it is a question of the existence of this nation. I am a Union man—a man who believes in the preservation of the Constitution and the laws of the land. And I tell you, if any Democrat tries to drag me into the support of measures that would impair the Union, he will find that he has mistaken his man.”

**WHO DID GO TO PRAIRIE IE GROVE?**

Vicksburg, Oct. 2d. —A letter from a prominent Union citizen of Bolivar county, Miss., says that Governor Brown of Georgia, with a considerable number of persons, visited Prairie le Grove on the 28th ultimo, on a mission to influence the citizens of that neighborhood to abandon the Union cause. The letter conjectures that they were sent from Richmond.

**The Fate of Charleston**

The New York Times says: “Gen. Gillmore telegraphs from Hilton Head that the work of reducing Charleston progresses slowly and surely. The bombardment will be continued, and indications show that the city will soon be brought within the range of our batteries. The inner harbor is entirely blockaded, and the rebel ironclads are held in check. The siege of Charleston is a matter of time only.”

**Every soldier is a statesman.** Every citizen of the United States, with his rights secured, is a statesman. This reminds us of a story told of old General Knox, who, when Governor Sullivan asked him why he held so much correspondence with the people, replied, “Because, sir, they are the sovereign power.” This is a truth which, saying it, every man should remember.

**COLORED TROOPS.**

The following remarks, from the Cincinnati Gazette, strike us as just and forcible:—

“The colored troops are compared to the rawest recruits. They have never had that training which other soldiers have had—they have never had the same advantages—and yet they fight as bravely, and are as willing to sacrifice their lives for their country as any of our white soldiers. They have a double duty to perform—they must, first, prove their courage and loyalty as soldiers, and then they must prove their manhood as citizens. If they succeed in both of these tasks, they will have earned a proud place in the history of this nation.”

**Important From the South.**

Washington, Oct. 12.—A dispatch from Hilton Head states that a large number of citizens of South Carolina, mostly women and children, have come into the Union lines, abandoning their homes and plantations. They report that they are being despoiled by the rebel guerillas. They express a desire to remain under Union protection. It is understood that Gen. Saxton has orders to provide for them.

**A call for volunteers** has been made in this State, to recruit the remaining regiments of colored troops allowed by the Government. Let every patriotic citizen assist in filling up these ranks. 

It makes no difference whether the condition of a man is that of the rich or the poor, of the educated or the unlearned, he is emphatically a man, and should not be made the subject of disparagement, for the sole reason that he was born of a race which has been subject to the degradation of slavery. Freedom, Equality, and Fraternity embody all the circumstances, and have equal claims to the affectionate regards and the just protection of the American citizen.

**COLORED REGIMENTS.**

The War Department has recently issued an order authorizing the organization of two additional regiments of colored troops in the State of Kansas. These regiments will be commanded by white officers. The enlistment is to be open to all colored men between the ages of 18 and 30. The bounty and pay will be the same as those offered to white soldiers. 

**A gentleman lately from New Orleans** states that the free colored people of that city are amongst the most loyal and patriotic citizens. They are actively engaged in supporting the Union cause, and are willing to contribute their time and money to the prosecution of the war.

**A Strange Story.**

A correspondent of the Philadelphia Press writes from Camp Parole, regarding a rebel who recently came in, and writing a letter to his brother at the South, begging him to come over to our side, and urging him to take his slaves with him. The brother replied that he would not think of it. The rebel wrote again, saying that he had been captured by a company of colored troops, and they had treated him so well that he was convinced the only hope of the Southern cause was to emancipate the slaves and enlist them in the Union army.
