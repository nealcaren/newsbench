SUFFRAGE WORKERS
THRONG CAPITOL TO
PLEAD FOR BALLOT

Take Possession of Hall of
House and Hold It
Through Hearing.

47 WORKING WOMEN
ANSWER ROLLCALL

Congressman Elect E. J. Hill
Sends Long Telegram
Expressing Sympathy.

[Special to The Courant]
Hartford, March 4.—The hall of the house of representatives was captured today by the suffragists, who sent the members into the senate chamber and proceeded to present a plea for woman suffrage before the joint committee on constitutional amendment.

It was just before noon when the suffragists, numbering nearly 200, marched into the hall and took possession. Mrs. Helen M. Shorey, president of the Connecticut Woman Suffrage association, read the rollcall of the members of the house, and as each name was called, a member of the suffrage group answered, "Here." Then 47 working women answered the rollcall. 

Representative Breen, who was presiding, protested, but the suffragists stood firm. Representative Hill then secured permission to address the crowd, and declared his sympathy with the movement. He handed Mrs. Shorey a telegram he had sent to Governor Baldwin and Senate President Davis, which follows:

“HARTFORD, March 4.—To Governor Baldwin and President Davis.
“The people of the Second Congressional District are deeply interested in the subject of woman suffrage. I have received thousands of letters and messages in favor thereof. Am in hearty sympathy with the extension of the franchise to women, and respectfully urge your consideration of the matter. 
“E. J. Hill.” 

The suffragists then began their hearing, stating their case for the extension of the franchise to women. Among those who spoke were Mrs. Shorey, Mrs. Mary J. Putnam, Miss Florence P. Harper and others. 

The hearing was continued for two hours, until the committee adjourned.

“In order to give you some proof of the general opinion on this subject,” said Mrs. Shorey, “we have canvasses made in this state and in other states embodying opinions, and we believe it will show that the sentiment for woman suffrage is rapidly growing. We also have petitions from over twenty towns in the state, signed by thousands of men and women, asking for the passage of a constitutional amendment granting suffrage to women.”

Mrs. Mary J. Putnam spoke of the vast number of women in the state who are engaged in industrial employment, and who are taxpayers. She said these women should have a voice in the laws that affect their lives. She declared that the granting of suffrage to women would add to the dignity of the home and would improve the standard of citizenship.

Miss Florence P. Harper spoke of the work that women are doing in the social, religious and educational fields, and said they should have a voice in the laws that affect these institutions.

RAPID GROWTH OF SENTIMENT.

“The sentiment for woman suffrage is growing rapidly in this country,” said Mrs. Shorey, “and it is only a matter of time until it will become a reality. The women of Connecticut have made a determined fight for the passage of a constitutional amendment, and we are confident that we will succeed in the end.”

Mrs. Shorey read a list of the names of the women who answered the rollcall. 

Miss Kaspar, a teacher, said she was present to state that the women of the state deserve the right to be heard in the making of laws.

Miss Anne Wakley, a nurse, said women are overworked and underpaid, and the ballot would help to remedy these economic wrongs. 

Others who spoke were Mrs. Elizabeth Meeker, Mrs. Alice L. Hennessy, Mrs. Flora A. Knapp, Mrs. Caroline E. Lewis, and Mrs. Florence L. Lyon.

TWO MEN KILLED
BY ROCKVILLE CAR

Smashes Into Buggy–Fleisch-
er and Richard Samich
Dead.

[Special to The Courant]
Rockville, March 3.—Two men, Louis Fischer and Richard Samich, were killed last night when an inbound trolley car struck a buggy in which they were riding, near the Rockville High school. The accident occurred at 7:30 o’clock. 

The victims lived on Pine street and were returning home after visiting a friend. The driver of the car, Frederick H. Wright, said he did not see the buggy until it was almost upon him, and that he applied the brakes, but it was too late to stop the car.

The buggy was smashed to splinters, and the two men were thrown from their seats. Dr. Amos W. Baker was summoned and pronounced Fischer and Samich dead at the scene.

The town was shocked by the tragedy. The men were well known and highly respected. Fischer was a retired business man and Samich was employed at the Rockville Woolen mill. 

The coroner, Dr. George B. Griffin, was notified, and an inquest will be held tomorrow.

KEEPING CLEAN AND WELL
By Dr. Elmer E. Homan, Physician.

The subject of personal hygiene is one that every man and woman should understand from childhood up. A great deal is heard of public health measures, such as clean milk, modern sewerage systems and the control of epidemics, but merely having all of these things does not guarantee, either to the individual or the community, a high standard of health. 

A great deal depends on the individual’s habits and practices, the things he does and the things he fails to do. Simple things often have a tremendous effect on the health. 

The body is covered with approximately two million glands which give off waste matters. These are best eliminated by the skin through perspiration. But to allow the body to become coated with dirt and grease interferes with the action of these glands and prevents the skin from performing its function properly. 

Therefore, the daily bath, toothbrushing and other means of removing dirt and disease germs are of great importance. 

Another thing that is often overlooked is the importance of proper oral hygiene. The mouth is the gateway to the body, and the germs from decayed teeth irritate the whole system. 

Many diseases start in the mouth. Toothbrushing after each meal is ideal, because it removes the particles of food left in the mouth. The use of dental floss is also advisable, because many kinds of food debris become lodged between the teeth.
