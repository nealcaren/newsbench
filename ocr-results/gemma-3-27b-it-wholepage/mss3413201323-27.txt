**THE REGISTER AND LEADER—WEDNESDAY MORNING, MAY 24, 1916**

**The Des Moines Register**

**TO THE MEMBERS OF THE LEGISLATURE**
We have been getting passionate communications from all over the state, the burden of which is a demand for a more thorough investigation into the management of the State Home for the Feeble-Minded at Glenwood. The complaints are of a serious nature, and, if true, speak of flagrant abuses. The members of the legislature, owing to the fact that they are away from home, are not in position to make the investigation themselves. They have delegated that duty to a committee. We would suggest to that committee that it make a thorough and impartial investigation, visit the institution without announcing any intention to do so, and interview the patients and attendants separately. It will pay to spend the time, do the work well and bring back a report that will satisfy the people of Iowa that the suffering people at Glenwood are being treated humanely and that the duties of the state are being properly discharged.

**THE CHALLENGE TO AMERICANISM**
In the first line that the “American” citizen—the one indigenous to the soil—must accept as his lot today, is that of demonstrating the superiority of his civic institutions. His claim to have originated democratic ideals dies when he suffers them to be rendered a mockery by those who seek to exploit them.  Sincerely, he cannot pose as the champion of democracy while an answer? What would we think of a nation that insists upon its existence being dependent upon subscribing to an antiquated and aristocratic code of ethics? 
The melancholy truth is that this country has been prostituting its ideals by over-encouraging immigration from lands where the very elements that built up the civilization of America are regarded with suspicion. It is not a question of race-prejudice or religious bigotry. It is a question of civic responsibility. The immigrant must prove that he can assume the obligations of American citizenship before he is given the privileges. He must learn our form of government and accept our standard of civic fidelity. 
It seems to us that this country underestimates the value of its own citizenship. A country that is willing to take any and all comers is sure to suffer a lowering of the standard of civic excellence. There is danger of being over-run by those who seek to undermine our institutions. 

**WE MUST BUILD FOR PERMANENCE**
The recent floods revisited the Mississippi valley, laid bare the folly of attempting to contend with nature in a half-hearted way. Radical and comprehensive plans are needed for the permanent control of the Mississippi river. Such projects are costly, but the cost of the damage from floods is greater.  Let the river be harnessed, its currents directed, its banks protected.  It is good business, as well as a matter of public safety.

**WHAT WE ARE FIGHTING FOR**
If, in this war, we should fail to defeat Germany, the result would be the triumph of autocracy, the extinguishing of liberty, and a reversion to the old order. Such a result would be a calamity to the world.  We must fight on to a finish—to a finish that guarantees the destruction of Prussianism and the triumph of democratic ideals. 

**LOCAL AND OTHERWISE**
The day is here when the tourist’s automobile is being more considered than the farmer’s buggy. 
The automobile has become a household necessity, but it is still a luxury. 
The fellow who can’t see a good thing when he sees it can’t see anything. 
The man who doesn't believe in preparedness hasn't been very widely acquainted.

**REPUBLICAN PLATFORM**
Adopted by the Republican State Convention at Des Moines, May 23, 1916.

We, the Republicans of Iowa, in convention assembled, reaffirm our loyalty to the principles of the party of Lincoln, and pledge ourselves to an honest and efficient administration of the affairs of state.

**Preamble**

Under the leadership of the Republican party the state of Iowa has made substantial progress in wealth, population, education, and industry. We rejoice in the prosperity of our people and we point with pride to the record of our party in building up the great state entrusted to our care. 

**Principles**

We believe in the supremacy of the law; in the preservation of our national honor; in a strict construction of the constitution; in the protection of the rights of all citizens; in equal opportunity for all; in honest money; in a stable currency; in the protection of domestic industries; in the preservation of our natural resources; in the promotion of education; in the welfare of the farmer, the laborer, and the business man.

**State Government**

We believe in a just and equitable system of taxation, and we favor a reduction in the rate of taxation whenever it can be done without impairing the efficiency of state government. We favor a non-partisan tax commission to revise the assessment of property for taxation purposes.

We believe in the efficient administration of state government, and we favor a system of civil service reform that shall place employees in office on the basis of merit and fitness.

**Education**

We believe that education is the foundation of a free government, and we favor a liberal support of the public schools of the state. We favor the establishment of a state university that shall be second to none in the nation.

**Agriculture**

We believe that agriculture is the most important industry in the state, and we favor the enactment of laws that will protect the interests of the farmer. We favor the establishment of a state experiment station that shall conduct research in the field of agriculture and disseminate information to the farmers of the state.

**Labor**

We believe that the laborer is entitled to a just and fair wage for his labor, and we favor the enactment of laws that will protect the rights of the workingman. We favor the establishment of a state bureau of labor that shall investigate the conditions of labor in the state and make recommendations for the improvement of those conditions.

**Prohibition**
We believe that the traffic in intoxicating liquors is a curse to the state, and we favor the enactment of a state-wide prohibition law.

**Suffrage**
We believe that women are entitled to the same rights and privileges as men, and we favor the extension of suffrage to women.

**WOMEN SUFFRAGE**
Women Suffragists to Make Vigorous Campaign for the Nomination of Candidates Who Favor Their Cause—A State-Wide Organization to Be Formed.

Des Moines, May 23.—Suffragists throughout Iowa are preparing for an active campaign this fall to secure the nomination of candidates for the legislature who will favor the submission of a constitutional amendment granting women suffrage. 

Mrs. George W. Clark, president of the Iowa Equal Suffrage association, announced today that committees have been organized in every county of the state to circulate petitions, hold meetings, and educate the voters on the question of suffrage. 

“We are confident that we will be able to secure a majority of the members of the legislature who will vote to submit the amendment,” said Mrs. Clark. “We have already received assurances from a number of candidates that they will support our cause.”

The state convention of the Iowa Equal Suffrage association will be held in Des Moines on June 15 and 16. At that time, plans will be completed for the fall campaign.

**WHEN JOHN P. IRISH FAVORED SUFFRAGE**
In the constitutional convention of 1875, when an amendment proposing woman's suffrage was presented, the discussion seemed to open the eyes of convention to the fact that the subject was hardly understood. Senator John P. Irish, then a young delegate, surprised followers by taking a bold and conspicuous position in favor of the measure.  What prompted him to do so, he explained to a friend after the convention adjourned.  

“I had just been listening to an address by Mrs. Elizabeth Cady Stanton,” he said. “She was a woman of remarkable ability and force of character. She presented her cause with a logic and clarity that were irresistible. I came to the conclusion that she—and the women she represented—deserved a fair hearing.”

The amendment was defeated, the vote standing 60 to 39. But the fact that John P. Irish, a prominent young delegate, had risen in defense of suffrage was significant. It was a sign that the cause was gaining ground, even in conservative circles. 

Later, in 1894, when the question of suffrage again came before the legislature, Mr. Irish was a member of the senate. He voted in favor of submitting the amendment to the voters of the state. 

He had told his friend, “I must admit to an intense admiration for the steady purpose and singular devotion to principle which the women of the suffrage movement have shown---a devotion which I believe is surpassed by no other. The measure of civil rights which they ask is no more than a recognition of the self-respecting and practical ideals of a great mass of women who bear their full share of the burdens and dangers of life. I believe that an intelligent portrayal of these ideals justifies a serious consideration of this question by the legislature.” 

Since then it has been noted that no man has ever expressed more genuinely or more eloquently a recognition of the fundamental equities involved in the cause of woman suffrage. Mr. Irish's views have been a source of inspiration for suffragists, and they have helped to advance the cause of suffrage in Iowa.

**VOTE "YES" ON THE ROAD BOND ISSUE**
The proposed $60,000,000 road bond issue will be submitted to the voters of Iowa on June 5th. This is a big proposition and deserves the careful consideration of every taxpayer of the state. 

Iowa needs good roads.  The fact is undeniable.  Our present road system is inadequate, and it is preventing the development of our state. Good roads are essential to the prosperity of the farmer, the business man, and the merchant.

The proposed bond issue will provide funds for the construction of a state-wide system of paved roads. These roads will connect the cities and towns of the state and will provide a smooth, safe, and convenient means of transportation for all.

The bonds will be paid off over a period of years, and the cost will be distributed over the entire state.  Every taxpayer will pay his share, but the benefits will be shared by all.

We urge all voters of Iowa to vote “Yes” on the road bond issue.  It is an investment in the future of our state.
