**ANTI-SUFFRAGE NOTES**

No. 169

Mrs. Robert Lansing, wife of the Secretary of State, accepts position as Secretary of the National Anti-Suffrage Association.

Elihu Root, America’s greatest statesman, presides at big Anti-Suffrage meeting in Utica.

Maine soldiers vote overwhelmingly against Woman Suffrage.

Officers of Connecticut Suffrage Association resign to join the “Militants.”

The annual convention, held in Washington, of the National Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage was singularly successful. It was attended by delegates from twenty-six states, and there was a widespread agreement of opinion among those present. It is difficult to estimate the number of women who are opposed to suffrage, but it is safe to say that they constitute a silent majority. 

Mrs. Robert Lansing, wife of the Secretary of State, has accepted the position of Secretary of the National Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage, and her acceptance, it is understood, is in complete harmony with the views of the Secretary of State. 

Elihu Root Presides at Utica Meeting—Urges Men to do Their Duty—Speaks of the Radical Character of the Suffrage Movement—Rebukes those who would Disrupt Home Life.

Utica, N. Y., Oct. 16.—The largest anti-suffrage meeting ever held in the city occurred here tonight, when a large audience filled the Auditorium to hear Elihu Root preside and address the gathering.  Mr. Root spoke with his usual force and eloquence, urging the men of the country to do their duty and oppose the extension of the franchise to women. He said in part:

"The suggestion that women need the ballot to right the wrongs that afflict them is a slander upon the men of the country.  The men of the country are well enough aware of the wrongs that afflict women, and they are doing everything in their power to remedy them.

“This movement is not a domestic movement; it is a radical movement. It is not a movement to improve conditions for women; it is a movement to destroy the home. Women are called upon to forsake their duties as wives and mothers and to enter the political arena, and this is a disservice to the country.”

Mr. Root was greeted with prolonged applause.  Other speakers included Mrs. Arthur Dodge and Dr. Rowena J. Crawford.

**REMEMBER MAINE VOTERS:**

**And Do the Same**

The vote in Maine on the Woman Suffrage Amendment was 66,194 against and 45,287 for.

The Anti-Suffrage women of Maine have rendered a great service to the state and to the country.  They organized early and worked tirelessly to educate the voters as to the dangers of granting suffrage to women.  Their efforts were rewarded by a decisive victory, and the state of Maine is to be commended for its sound judgment.

The men of Maine deserve great credit for standing by their wives, mothers, sisters and daughters and for upholding the principles of American home life.  Their vote is a warning to the suffragists that their schemes will not succeed in Maine.

***

The attempts of the so-called “militants” to disrupt the proceedings of the National Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage were, fortunately, not very successful.  Their disorderly tactics and their unladylike conduct met with the disapproval of the great majority of those in attendance. The fact that the “militants” activity is confined to a small number of women is a cause for satisfaction to those who believe in the proper methods of agitation.

The militants who have thus far appeared in the east have come from outside of New York State—from Pennsylvania, Connecticut and other states—and their presence is interpreted as evidence that the suffrage cause is in a steady decline.

The National Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage has issued a statement congratulating the women and men of Maine on their victory in the recent referendum on the Woman Suffrage Amendment.  The statement says in part:

“The result of the election in Maine demonstrates that the people of this country are not willing to surrender the principles of American home life to the suffragists. It is a victory for the home, for the family, and for the true welfare of women.”

***

The Republicans of the New York State Senate and Assembly who voted against the Woman Suffrage Amendment in the recent campaign are receiving numerous expressions of commendation from all over the state.  It is said that many of them are already being spoken of as candidates for re-election.

***

The suffragists are making a desperate attempt to win the support of the farmers.  They are urging the farmers’ wives to vote for suffrage, arguing that it will enable them to obtain better prices for their products.  However, the farmers are not being deceived by this argument.  They know that the prices of farm products are determined by the laws of supply and demand, and that the ballot will not affect those laws.

***

It is reported that a large number of women who formerly supported the suffrage movement are now openly opposing it.  These women say that they have become convinced that suffrage is not in the best interests of women or of the country.

***

Mrs. Medill McCormick, a prominent leader in the anti-suffrage movement, delivered a stirring address before the Women’s Republican Club of Chicago this week.  She argued that suffrage would lead to the degradation of women and the destruction of the home.

Officers of the Connecticut Suffrage Association Resign—Adopt Militant Tactics—Demand Recognition from National Headquarters

New Haven, Conn., Oct 15.—Following a heated discussion at a meeting held here last night, five officers of the Connecticut Suffrage Association have resigned to join the “Militants,” as they term themselves.  The resignations were presented in the form of a resolution stating that the association had not been sufficiently aggressive in its methods and that a more radical policy was necessary to achieve success.

The newly formed “Militant” group has announced its intention to follow the tactics of the English suffragettes and to engage in all forms of protest, including picketing, parades and demonstrations.  They also demand recognition from the National suffrage headquarters.  

The resignation of the officers has caused a considerable stir in suffrage circles in Connecticut.  Many members of the association are opposed to the militant tactics and fear that they will alienate public sympathy.
