It is not a question of conceding to women a right they have never possessed, but a question of depriving men of a right they have always possessed—the right to choose their representatives. 

It is not a question of removing disabilities, for women are not disabled by the laws of the state. On the contrary, they are protected by the laws. 

It is a question of the right of the sex that does not bear arms, does not pay taxes, and does not hold office, to dictate the policies of the government. 

The present representative system presupposes that those who make the laws shall be those who are subject to them. To give women the right to vote is to strike at the very foundation of our government.

A man may hold property and pay taxes, is subject to military duty and all the other burdens of citizenship; he is a part of the structure of the state and the extension of the franchise to him is a logical right. A woman is not subject to military duty; she does not pay taxes; she does not hold office. To give her the right to vote is to give her an advantage and a power that she is not entitled to possess. The constitution provides equal protection of the laws to all citizens. This is all the political right to which a woman is entitled. It is not just that any consideration should be shown in extending the franchise be yond the men who bear the burdens of the nation.
