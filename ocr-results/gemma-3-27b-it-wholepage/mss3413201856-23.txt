Assassination of Hon. Ebenezer K. Brooks, in Salem—Additional Particulars—
The Excitement Aroused—The Arrest of the Assassin—His Motives—
Next of Jacob Hebert—The Circumstances of the Tragedy—
Claimed to be Insane—His Condition—
The Coroner’s Inquest—Verdict—
The Funeral Arrangements

SALEM, August 22—The circumstances surrounding the assassination of Hon. Ebenezer K. Brooks yesterday is, if possible, more shocking and appalling than first described. The deed was done at half-past eight o’clock, just as Mr. Brooks was entering his own door, on his way to breakfast. He was accompanied by his daughter, Miss Louisa Brooks, who was nearly overcome, but did not receive a physical injury nor witness the actual stabbing beyond a glimpse of the assailant’s arm uplifted. The assassin, Jacob Hebert, had been lurking around Mr. Brooks’ house for several days, and had made several threatening remarks to people in the neighborhood concerning the deceased. It appears that Mr. Brooks had several months since commenced an action against Hebert to recover a large sum of money which Hebert had borrowed to purchase property. The case was to be tried on Monday next, and it is said that Hebert had constantly expressed his fear of ruin if he lost the case.
The funeral will take place on Sunday afternoon, at half-past two o’clock.
It is claimed by the friends of Hebert that he is insane, and was in a state of partial delusion at the time of the assault.

CITY OF ROCHESTER,
August 21—The Rochester Daily Advertiser gives the following particulars of the attempt to assassinate Hon. E. K. Brooks:
The attempted assassination of Hon. E. K. Brooks occurred this morning, near his residence, on Essex Street. Mr. Brooks was walking with his daughter, when he was suddenly attacked by a man named Jacob Herbert, with a long, sharp knife. Herbert stabbed Mr. Brooks in the left side, inflicting a dangerous wound. Mr. Brooks, though severely wounded, managed to grapple with his assailant, and with the assistance of his daughter, disarmed him. Herbert was immediately arrested.
Mr. Brooks was taken to his residence, where he received medical attention. He is resting quietly, but his condition is serious. It is feared the wound is mortal.
The motive for the attack is believed to be a legal dispute that existed between Mr. Brooks and Herbert. Mr. Brooks was the attorney for the plaintiff in a case involving a large sum of money, and Herbert was the defendant. The case was to be tried next week, and it is said that Herbert had made threats against Mr. Brooks.
The coroner’s inquest was held this afternoon, and the jury returned a verdict that Mr. Brooks died from the effects of a wound inflicted by Jacob Herbert.

WASHINGTON NOTES.
The San Francisco Telegram states that the Senate Committee on Naval Affairs have agreed to report favorably the bill authorising the construction of a new monitor for the Pacific squadron.

THE “VISITATION” CONTROVERSY.
The recent act of the Mayor of Boston in prohibiting the Smith’s Ophthalmic Institute, otherwise known as the “Visitation,” from operating within the limits of the city, has created an unnecessary excitement. The society consists of well-meaning people, who think they are doing a good work. But the statement by the Board of Health, that the operation inflicted more harm than good, is a strong argument against the Institution and represents the opinion of members of the medical profession, and it is therefore not without reason that the Mayor has forbidden them to practice within the city. This matter has attracted a great deal of attention, and may lead to the investigation of the methods of other so-called “healing” institutions. It is a well known fact that fraudulent and irresponsible agencies are constantly imposing upon the credulous, and it is certainly a wise precaution for the national and municipal authorities to take steps to prevent these abuses.

MISSOUURI PHILLIPS’S EDITION.
The St. Louis Globe-Democrat publishes the following instance of the brazenness of the Missouri Phillips edition of Shakespeare:
“The Missouri edition of Shakespeare, as published by Phillips, some time since, has been known to have been notoriously inaccurate, and even to have been subject to the charge of deliberate misrepresentation of the text. The following paragraph, taken from a recent critical article in the New York Tribune, is a further illustration of the bad faith of the enterprise. The Tribune says:
‘The Missouri Shakespeare is not merely an incorrect but a mutilated edition. In the play of King John, which we took up for examination, omissions are at least as frequent as errors. No less than 187 lines are dropped from the text, and among these are some of the most beautiful as well as most important passages in the play. This is not editing; it is destruction. It is not a mere matter of errors of print and oversight; it is a deliberate and consistent simplification of Shakespeare’s language and thought and it indicates not a poverty of the text but a poverty of editorial capacity.’”

The Business World.

THE FINANCIAL SITUATION.
NEW YORK, August 22—The financial situation remains unsettled. The trouble commences with the disturbances of the past, with the opening of the London market. The Bank of England increased its rate of discount to 3%. The gold market opened at 115, but closed at 114 1/2. Government bonds were steady. Railroad stocks opened with a decline, and remained so throughout the day. The failure of Jay Cooke & Co. has caused a great shock to the business community. There is a general feeling of uncertainty throughout the market.

FOREIGN INTELLIGENCE.

THE SPANISH REVOLUTION.
MADRID, August 22—The Spanish revolution continues. The rebels are advancing on Madrid. The government forces are making a determined resistance. The situation is critical. The latest reports indicate that the rebels have captured several towns in the province of Madrid.
