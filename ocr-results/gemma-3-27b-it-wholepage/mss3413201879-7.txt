Ella M. Stewart Speaks Before League of Women Voters.
At a luncheon of the League of Women Voters of New York City held at the home of Mrs. George Schenck, of Syvania, Mrs. Ella M. Stewart, of Pennsylvania, who has been spending a few days in the city in the interest of industrial legislation, spoke of the opportunities open to women in politics. Mrs. Stewart, a representative of the Women's Trade Union League, took up the retail trades situation of New York State, and the urgent need of protective legislation against overlong hours and low wages. 

She spoke of the necessity of repeating the laws and of the need of women's organizations encouraging public officials to coöperate. The League of Women Voters according to Mrs. Stewart, can do more to secure the passage of desired laws than any other organization. This is because they are composed of women who are interested in creating a better social and economic order.

Mrs. Stewart spoke of the effort to secure the passage of the thirty-hour week for women law in New York state. 

Mrs. Henry Moskowitz presided at the luncheon and introduced the speakers. Other guests present included Mrs. George Schenck, Mrs. Charles Lewis, Mrs. Lillian Wald, Mrs. Helen Hoy, and Miss Amy Woolworth.

**New Jersey Women Workers Hold Mass Meetings.**

A series of mass meetings of women workers were held in New Jersey during the past week, under the auspices of the New Jersey State Federation of Women’s Clubs, for “The Woman in Industry” program.

The first meeting was held in Passaic, where a large number of women workers were present, and a speaker from the State Federation gave an address on the protective laws for women, and the necessity for an active organization to enforce those laws, and to secure additional legislation for the welfare of women workers.

The second meeting was held in Paterson, where a large gathering of women textile workers was present. The speaker explained at length the advantages of joining the organized labor movement. 

At Newark, a luncheon was arranged for the women workers. Mrs. Mary Sweeney, of the Industrial Welfare Committee of the State Federation of Women’s Clubs, spoke, and Miss Florence Howell, of the State Labor Department, answered a number of questions.

The movement is gaining momentum in New Jersey, and it is believed that a strong organization of women workers will be formed soon to advocate their rights in the various industries of the state.

**New Jersey Women Workers Present Argument to Legislators**

The wage-earning women of New Jersey appeared in force before the Industrial Relations Committee of the legislature to advocate the passage of a bill providing for an equal pay act. They were led by Mrs. George M. Kibble, president of the New Jersey State Federation of Women’s Clubs.

The women presented a carefully prepared argument explaining the injustice to women who are forced to work for lower wages than men for the same work. They maintained that such discrimination is unfair and leads to economic dependence and social inequality.

In the course of her statement Mrs. Kibble said that "the right to earn a decent living is a fundamental right of every citizen, regardless of sex." She also pointed out that women are now an essential part of the industrial work force, and that they are entitled to the same consideration as men.

The committee listened attentively to the women’s arguments, and promised to give the bill careful consideration. 

**Equal Pay Question Begun in Toledo**

Toledo, O. – A move to secure equal pay for equal work, for women employed by the city, has begun here. A petition bearing 250 signatures, mostly women employes of the city government, has been presented to the city council.

The petition asks that women be paid the same as men for the same kind of work, and says that the present wage scale is unjust and leads to hardships for women workers.

The petition has been referred to the finance committee, which will make a report at the next meeting of the city council.

**Director of Federal Bureau Gives Views**

"The approval of an equal pay bill by the legislature would be a moral victory for all women workers," said Miss Mary Anderson, director of the Women’s Bureau of the U.S. Department of Labor, when interviewed at her office here today. "The struggle for economic equality is one of the most important issues of the day, and it is essential that women be given an equal opportunity to earn a decent living. 

"The fact that women are now earning less than men for the same work is a clear indication of the discrimination that exists in our industrial system. This discrimination is not only unfair to women, but it also has a harmful effect on the economy as a whole. 

"When women are paid less than men, they have less money to spend, which means that there is less demand for goods and services. This leads to a slowdown in economic activity and ultimately hurts everyone."

Miss Anderson also said that the passage of an equal pay bill would help to improve the lives of women workers and their families. 

"When women are paid a decent wage, they are able to provide their children with better education, better healthcare, and better opportunities," she said. "This will help to build a stronger and more prosperous society for all."

**Textile Workers’ Union Discusses Laws**

The Local Textile Workers’ Union, at a recent meeting, discussed the need for more protective laws for women in the textile industry. Several of the members spoke of the hardships they have suffered because of long hours and low wages. It was decided to send a committee to the legislature to ask for the passage of a bill providing for an eight-hour day for women workers in the textile industry. The committee was instructed to also ask for a minimum wage law for women in the textile industry.
