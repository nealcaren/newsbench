A SONG WRITER CELEBRITY.

ENTHRALLED BY THE MELO-
DY OF HIS OWN RACE—THE
SUCCESS HE HAS WON.

In His Family He Has Won the
Esteem of the Those Who Know
Him Best.

Issued from the editor’s sanctum this
week a tribute to a noted composer, but not
a composer of the white blood. A copy of
the article is here reproduced, says the New
York Gleaner, and success is especially interesting in those
who overcome seemingly insurmountable ob-
stacles. The subject is Samuel Snaith, whose
name may not be known to many, though his
songs are sung in every church and school
throughout the land. The relative of George
Washington (for be it known he traces his
ancestry to the hero of the revolution), Mr.
Snaith is a man of color, born in a rural dis-
trict of Virginia. His mother was a slave.
The son inherits a rich musical legacy from
his mother, whose songs were the joy of all
the slaves for miles around. The talent was
transferred to the son, and the poem, “The
Old Log Cabin,” which his mother taught him
in infancy, inspired his first attempt at com-
position—a sacred cantata, “A Dream of
Home.” This was written when he was but
ten years old. From that time until now he
has been constantly composing, and is now
the author of some three hundred songs, many
of which have already become popular. His
compositions are marked by an originality
that is rare, and a depth of feeling that
touches the heart. He writes both sacred and
secular songs, and his works are filled with
a love of nature, a reverence for God, and a
sympathy for the struggling masses. He is a
member of the American Society of Composers,
Authors and Publishers, and is a director of
that organization.

It is in some pleasant places where there
are colored people that a feeling reigns
that the colored race is forced to work
and struggle for an exception which el-

“It matters not,” said Mr. Snaith, “how
much people may assume. It is useless if
they are not doing—and doing it right—
the thing which is expected of them. So
many are colored men and women, just
as I am, who have not had the same op-
portunity that I have had to constantly
cultivate and daily work at their profes-
sion. I have the honor of being a mem-
ber of St. Phillip’s P. E. church. I be-
lieve the Church of God is the best in-
stitution on earth, and that it ought to
be the leader of political and social re-
form. I have given myself to Christian
work, and hold a position as leader in
the Sunday school and the F. A. M. As-
sociations.”

[IMAGE: Illustration of a forest lane, labeled "Forest Lane Farm."]

Mr. Snaith has a warm and generous
disposition, and is always ready to assist
those in need. He is a devout Christian
and a consistent member of the Presbyterian
church. His home is a model of comfort
and refinement, and he is surrounded by a
loving family. In his success we see a
demonstration of what can be achieved by
industry, perseverance and faith. He is a
credit to his race, and an example to all
who aspire to achieve success, no matter
what obstacles may lie in their way.

LOCAL NEWS. 

CHARITABLE WORK.

TO AID THE NEEDY.

The Ladies’ Industrial School association
will meet to-morrow afternoon at the school
building on Locke street, at 2:30 o’clock.
The object of the meeting is to map out the
work for the coming winter, which will be
largely to provide clothing and food for the
needy families of the city. It is hoped that
a large attendance will be present, and that
a generous response will be given to the call
for assistance.

A PLEASANT RECEPTION.

Mrs. W. A. Jernigan entertained a number
of friends at her home on Fletcher avenue
last evening. The guests spent the evening
in social conversation and music. Elegant
refreshments were served.

THE HOLIDAY PROGRAM.

The members of the Second Baptist church
will give a pleasing holiday program on next
Sunday evening. A feature of the program
will be a Christmas tree and a distribution
of gifts to the children of the congregation.
