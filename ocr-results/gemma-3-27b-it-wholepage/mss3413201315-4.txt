**The “Better” City Government.**

The Boulder, Colo., city charter gives the managing director of the city “gener- al supervision of all the city's affairs.” and this allows him to hire and fire all city officials, even members of the city council. This has been declared unconstitutional in Colorado. It is a striking illustration of how the city managers’ plan would reduce the voters to impotence. But the city managers, the advocates of the commission form of government, assure us that they are seeking to make it safe for democracy! In fact, they merely strip all power from the voters. The entire control of civic affairs would be concentrated in a small group, who would be responsible to no one, and would jeopardize the government that it takes for years to build. Even the city managers themselves could not weather the storm of public disapproval. 

Why are these men, who are supposed to be practical, working so hard to curtail the very life of self-government? The answer, of course, is that they are all, without exception, of the “short-hair” type, city planners, engineers, and men who think they know every- thing about governing, but precious little about human nature. 

**AS MONTANA SEES MISS RANKIN.**
Miss Jeannette Rankin, the suffragists’ star, is being freely discussed in Montana. The Helena Record says: 
“Miss Rankin—a woman who thinks she has a mission, and who is going to ‘uplift’ Montana and the nation—had the temerity to address a crowd of people in Chicago. She came to Chicago to make a speech, to preach her doctrine of universal suffrage, and whatever she did or said resulted in one man being slugged.
“But from the dispatches it seems that the slugger did Miss Rankin a favor, because she was so scared that she forgot what she was going to say and turned tail and ran. She is said to have looked back over her shoulder several times to see if the man who slugged his friend was coming after her. She told the crowd that she did not ‘know if it was right for a woman to vote.’ It is a fair assumption that it is not right for a woman—or ‘any human being’—to go around dragging a slugger along with her wherever she goes.”

**The first prison for women.**

The first prison for women was opened at Sherbrooke, Canada. What the suffragists call the “new woman” seems to easily drift toward lawlessness. It is good to know that some provision is being made for these lost members of society. 

**Mrs. W. E. Brickner’s Views.**

Mrs. W. E. Brickner, president of the New York State Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage, has sent out a letter asking for financial assistance to help continue the fight against votes for women. She says: 
“When women become voters they will necessarily divide along party lines. This means that the home will be disrupted. Husband and wife will be encouraged to disagree on political issues. Such discord has already shown itself in states where women vote. This is not a theoretical objection. It is based on actual observation." 

**The Man’s Vote.**

The practical value of the ballot rests on the principle of responsibility. It is a power best exercised by those who bear the burdens of actual service. The man goes to war, and it is the man who pays the bills. Responsibility rests upon his shoulders and in granting him the ballot it also acknowledges his responsibility. In giving the ballot to women we have failed to guarantee equal responsibility. 

A letter from a soldier in France writes: “I’m willing a woman should have a vote when she is willing to be drafted and fight for her country."

**The Children’s Burden.**

The official figures show that in States where women have the franchise the num- ber of working girl children have increased. In Illinois, where women have partial suffrage, 27,708 girls under sixteen years of age are employed. That is no part of the "emancipation" women want.

**The Coming Menace**
The National Woman’s party has endorsed the revolutionary industrial plan known as industrial unionism. This unionism has been condemned by many of the leading industrial and economic authorities in the country. It strikes at the foundation of organized industry, and under its leadership general unrest is spreading. The women have stepped into a role of leadership that is fraught with danger to the country. 

**Woman’s Place**
Woman’s place is to make the home the center of life and society, to train the children in virtue and patriotism, to encourage the man in his duties and to offer him the support this country is to enjoy other conditions. She is the guardian of the family and lives and the preserver of the nation. Statesmanship, diplomacy, and the business of governing should be left to men. 

**Report of the Executive Committee**
The executive committee of the New York State Association Opposed to Woman Suffrage met at the Hotel Commodore on October 25th. Addresses were made by Mrs. George C. Boldt and Miss Edith M. Baxter. 

It was voted to continue the organization of anti-suffrage societies throughout the State. 

It was voted to send circular letters to all club women, urging them to oppose the passage of the suffrage amendment. 

A committee was appointed to prepare a brief outlining the unsoundness of the suffrage movement.

***

**Patriotism & Protection**

The true patriotism of woman is a devotion to home and family, and in the preservation of the nation’s virtues. It is the protection of those who cannot protect themselves—the young, the old, and the helpless. The ballot is not the only expression of citizenship!
