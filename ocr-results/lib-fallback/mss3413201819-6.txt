Phone Hook-up Joins 3 Cities

Tonight, in three widely separated cities Oberlin Alumni Associations will sit down before amplifiers at their respective meeting places and listen to a special telephone hook-up. Thru the special requests of the Alumni chapters of Omaha, Nebraska, Mineapolis, Minnesota, and New Haven, Connecticut, a program that will last between ten and fifteen minutes will be broadcast from the Dudley Allen Art building where the President's Reception will be in progress.

It has been planned that President Wilkins, Mr. Ralph Andrews of Buffalo, New York, President of the Alumni Association, and Mr. Earl Newsom will speak. Mr. Newsom is expected to recount some of the high points of the day. The program beginning at 9:30 will last until 9:45 p.m.

Little Brothers to Frolic in Arb

Oberlin "big brothers" and their little brothers from the Children's Home will go for an outing at the Arb next Sunday afternoon, Chairman Bob Gassler revealed yesterday. The outing will be the first step in a much-enlarged program for the little brothers this year, Gassler declared. Plans call for picnics, more outings, ball games, and parties for the children.

The big brother organization is a branch of the Young Men's Christian Association designed primarily to furnish outside activities to children in the Home. All upperclassmen and freshmen who have little brothers are urged to meet in the Men's Building before three o'clock on next Sunday.

FIELD DAY

Tomorrow afternoon at Dill Field the Freshmen, Sophomores will hold a Field Day, designed to replace the old time flag rushes and greased pole climbs. Instead of fighting for the possession of a battered flag, the two classes will compete in 100, 200, and 400 yard dashes, 110 yard low hurdles, 880 medley and straight relays, a cross country race, tennis matches, and a tug-of-war for all those not engaging in some other activity.

To be eligible freshmen must sign with their president John Hoagland, and Sophs with President Dack Forbush...The meet will be under the direction of Bob Smith, '38...The Freshman Band will play.

TEACHER'S FEDERATION URGES JAPANESE BOYCOTT

CHICAGO — Officials of the American Federation of Teachers announced today that their organization has endorsed a nationwide boycott of Japanese products and urged all Americans to join it.

"Let the leaders of Japan know that we hate war, that we detest aggressors, and that we know how to deal with them," said Dr. Geo. Artelle, vice-president of the federation.

Announce 2 Four Year Scholarships

Today, at the luncheon held for the delegates in the Warner Gymnasium, it was announced that several funds have been established for the benefit of Oberlin students.

The will of Miss Bertha B. McClymonds of Morris Plains, N. J., who died last spring, provides for two four-year scholarships of $450, each year These scholarships are for women only and will be the largest ever made available for Oberlin students. Miss McClymonds also made similar gifts to four other colleges. A lecture fund has also been established in memory of Miss Nellie Rose Heldt, ex-'33, who died in January 1936. This fund will provide for the giving and publication of lectures on humanitarian and scientific subjects.

Library Has Co-Ed Display

"Learning for Ladies' is the title of the exhibit arranged by the Oberlin College Library and placed on display in the ground floor show-cases. Over 25 pieces of literature are arranged in order of their dates to give a fairly complete history of coeducation since the Rennaisance Period.

Books. Newspapers

Besides these books and newspapers in the sequence, there are several interesting items relating to coeducation at Oberlin. Among this last group there is the original diploma awarded to the first co-ed to graduate from Oberlin, Caroline Rudd. Former Classes Sit Together At Alumni Banquet

All tickets for the Alumni Banquet being held tonight at the Oberlin Inn were sold out well in advance of the occasion. To take care of all those who wish to hear the speakers even though they will not be able to get in to the dinner, there will be amplifiers in the lobby through which the program will be broadcast.

The alumni at the banquet will be seated in a way that will enable old friends to get together to renew old acquaintances and talk over old times. They will be seated according to their respective classes.

Presiding over the dinner will be Mr. Ralph M. Andrews, president of the Alumni Association. The former lieutenant-governor of Ohio and a Democratic National Committeeman, Mr. Charles A. Sawyer will speak on the subject, "Women in Public Life". The other speaker will be Professor Robert S. Fletcher on the subject "Those First Co-Eds."
