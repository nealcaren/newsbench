THE PICTURE OF COL. SHAW IN BOSTON.
Buried with his negroes in the trench I—
There he lies, a score of them around him!
All the fires of bondage light that shroud!
Could a monument so well have crowned him!
Sight to make a father's bosom throb—
There he stands upon the canvas glowing!
Sight to make a noble mother sob—
Tender eyes, their glasses on her throwing!
There he stands, so eloquent and mute,
Modest, and yet looking in our faces,
Undisturbed and calmly, as doth suit
One who did not ask the world's high places!
There he gazes, soldier-like and bold,
Not a whit ashamed to die with him!
Hmm, the man of blood, bought and sold;
Not a bit ashamed to lie with him!
Look upon him, Nation of the free!
Surely this shall cure thee of thy meanness;
Look upon him, Nation, thou to be,
Crying out renunciant, "Oh, my leanness!"
Sleep serenely, with thy country's prog,
Noble martyr to the nation given!
With thy little company on high,
Thou shalt advertise all the plans of Heaven!
MARTHA PERRY LOWE.
Boston Daily Advertiser.

SONG OF TRIUMPH.
Shout! let the welkin ring!
Timbrel and trumpet bring,
The victory's won!
Let the loud cannon's roar
Publish, from shore to shore,
Now and forevermore
The work is done!
Let the wide-bounding bell
Far the glad story tell,
O'er land and sea;
High on the dome of night
Let the star rockets bright
Blazon in words of light,
"Columbia's free!"
Touch every tuneful string,
Wide your glad voices fling,
With bound refrain!
Let the red torches light
Flash on the started night,
Showing the freeman's might
In lengthened train.
Never shall slavery's chain
Clank on our dome domain—
One soul is free;
Free as the waters run—
Free for earth's toiling one—
Free for rude Africa's son—
Shout, Liberty!
