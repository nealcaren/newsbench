In June, 1912, the first Equal Suff-
rage meeting in Florida was called,
and about thirty women met in a
drawing room to organize. This was
at Jacksonville, and the Florida
Equal Franchise League was formed.
In less than one year this league had
aroused so much interest in Florida
that one of the leading attorneys and
statesmen took up their amendment
to the constitution granting full cit-
izenship to women and so success-
fully championed it that the whole
state was aroused and amazed at the
hold of the modern movement, “votes
for women," in the peninsular state.

Several women in mid-life, some
society girls, and the rest between
the two, took up the work, and now
ante-bellum ladies are enthusiasti-
cally urging the right to say for
themselves just who shall hold office
and what tax shall be levied on the
property that they own.

Even conservative Virginia began
in 1900 and other Southern states
followed, but Florida has taken suff-
rage on the flood tide, and has not
had to “educate" her people, for
Florida has a free and happy popu-
lation, people who have as a motto
“Live and let live,' and many of the
men are ready to say “Yes, come and
help us. We need you, and you have
never had the position that belongs
to you."

The officers elected at the first
meeting were Mrs. Katherine Living-
ston Eagan, president; Mrs. Roselle
C. Cooley, first vice-president; Mrs.
Herbert L. Anderson, second vice-
president; Mrs. E. M. Sanderson,
treasurer; Miss Frances Anderson,
corresponding secretary; Miss Ethel
McCoy, recording secretary. Mrs.
Eagan being far from well and going
to Paris to be with her family, left
Mrs. Cooley, the vice-president, to
preside. She is a native of Florida,
having a state-wide acquaintance.

and Mrs. Cooley had the able support

However, the women of Florida did
better than women in the other states
in coming through both committees
and having so many friends for the
bill in the first legislature in which
it was presented. We now have in
Florida suffrage leagues at Jackson-
ville, Orlando, Miami, Lake Helen,
Milton, Pensacola, Zellwood, Talla-
hassee, Tampa, Ruskin, Pine Castle
and Winter Park, hundreds of women
throughout the state, men friends of
equal rights all over the state, and
a Men's Suffrage League at Orlan-
do.

And why not? There is just one
point in suffrage. It is not ‘what
will women do with the vote, it is
not pre-suffrage promises, it is not
greater purity in living conditions,
it is only this: Has woman the same
right as man to the ballot? So far
no man or woman has given one
single reason why she has not.

The Florida Equal Franchise Lea-
gue hereby returns thanks to all per-
sons who have assisted to make this
Suffrage Issue of The State the
great success that it is, and asks
careful consideration of all thinking
people as to why women have been
for so many years set apart as a
class by themselves. Honestly now,
don't you think it strange? Women
are people, they are half of the Cau-
casian race, they do one-half the
work of the world, they train and ed-
ucate the boys for good citizenship.
Is there one good reason why they
may not cast a vote for the men who
are entrusted with the making of
the laws? We challenge any reader
of this paper to send us one good
reason.

WHY WOMEN SHOULD VOTE.

(rrom a man s standporrnt.)

First, I would say men and women
were created in the likeness of God
without discrimination or distinction.
Second, the expression of the right
of franchise or the ballot is a God-
given right and women have as good
right to it as men. A woman is
man's equal intellectually and often
his superior in intuitive instinct. In
questions of moral nature, her decis-
ions between the right and wrong of
a thing are nearer right than man's.
She needs the ballot for self-protec-
tion, a shield and weapon, when she
is left with the whole family depend-
ent on her. She should have the bal-
lot that she mighty enjoy the highest
freedom that civilization implies. The
psychological effect on generations to
come would be marvelous. To stop
the awful expense put into war prep-
arartions used to destroy mankind,
instead of civilizing and educating
him. To assist to operate a govern-
ment that approximates something
like justice to all men.

These are a few of the reasons
why women should have the ballot.

C. W. BALDWIN, St. Petersburg.

SUFFRAGE GAINS.

Charles M. Thomson, member of
the house from Illinois, is out with a
flat-footed declaration in favor of
woman suffrage. “There is not one
sound reason," said he, “that I have
ever heard advanced why women
should be denied the right to vote.
The real question is not why women
should have the ballot. The question
is why she should no have it." Much
more along the same lines Congress-
man Thomson said which shows that
he has a proper idea of the rights of
the people. He stated in unequivocal
terms that he would support the reso-
lution granting to women the right
to vote.

Congressman Sydney Anderson, of
Minnesota, says that the suffrage sen-
timent in his state is growing and he
believes that it is so strong that the
next Legislature will without doubt
submit the question to the people.
He added that the success of woman
suffrage in Norway had exerted a
great deal of influence in making
woman suffrage popular in Minne-
sota, where there are a great many
people of Norweigian descent.
