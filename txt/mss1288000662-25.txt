AMONG THE CLUBS
     After an illness
of two
weeks, Max H.
Newman is now
on the road to
recovery and 
may be reached
at his home, 24
Davis street,
Boston. To the
many clubs and 
members who
brought cheering words and messages
which helped immensely towards
encouraging him and helping 
to pass away dull hours, Mr. Newman
wishes to take this opportunity
of heartily thanking. He is glad
that soon he can be with the friends
he missed so poignantly during his
confinement, and once more be able 
to serve the clubs in a labor of love.
--THE JEWISH ADVOCATE.

     The Nordeau Judeans presented
the Roxbury Welfare Center with a
large portrait of the late Max Nordau.
The presentation speech was
made by Maurice Birnbach, president 
of the club.

     The Samsons rendered a very fine
presentation recently at the all-star
night at the Practical Arts School.
The playlet known as the Frolics of
a Dream, held the following characters:
Shylock, by Krop; Hamlet,
by Perlmutter; Herzl, by Stengel;
Lincoln, by Stiller; What Fools
these Mortals Be, by Farber, Berman
and Kaplan. Speilvogel gave
the prologue and Kridberg represented
youth.

     The third special meeting of the 
T. D. L. Association was held Thursday,
March 8, at Montague Hall,
Brooklyn, N. Y. All officers, organizers,
charter and initial members
were present. Meetings of this kind,
held every two years, are solely for
the purpose of electing officers. All
officers elected and appointed at the
first organization gathering in 1916,
in Roxbury, have since been re-elected
three times, and at present all 
hold original offices and are active 
promoters. A vote of confidence is
taken for these representatives every
year, and it has proved that ll votes
are unanimously favorable. The lawmaking
bodies of the T. D. L. Association,
unlike those of the majority
of the senior organizations in the
country, are not based on parliamentary
procedure. However, they do
closely follow upon the actual basis
principles and customs of this government.
The officials of the T. D. L.
are: Ralph Hannington, president;
Albert Salkind, director. Executives
of the Boston Order, are: Herbert J.
Stone, vice-president; Dr. H. Robert
Goldberg, secretary; Rederick Stein,
treasurer; Ralph L. Fishman, assistant
director; Jack Perley, assistant
to the president. New York Order,
Harold M. Salkind, chairman of the
board and publicity agent; Jesse
Kaufman, vice-president; Robert
Parker, treasurer; R. Matthew
Sterne, Jr., secretary; by honorary
appointment, Max H. Newman, special
correspondent.

     The Radio Boys of Dorchester
visited me and were very sorry that

radio set for my benefit.

     The Bartholdy Club held its instilation
and dance, March 7. It is conceded
to have been one of the most
brilliant affairs held by that club.
The installation was carried off
superbly through the able co-managering
of Mr. Arthur Goldstein and
Mr. Al Goldman. The club director,
Mr. Oppenheim, spoke a few well
chosen words in which he summed
up the tims of the club and of its
members. Mr. George Maren was installed
as president; Mr. Albert
Adelson as treasurer; and Mr. Solomon
Stern as secretary.

     A new resolution was passed by
the Roxbury Cubs at their recent
meeting to the effect that the members
not paying their fines should
be suspended from the club for a
number of weeks, the exact number
to be decided by the club.

     At the Amateur Night Show held
at the Sarah Greenwood School,
March 9th, the Corona club was
well represented by Goldman, Gross,
and Simberg. These boys had but a
very short notice in which to prepare,
yet they were chosen winners
for the second place. The Corona's
baseball team is open for challenges
by any 16-year-old club in Dorchester
or Roxbury. Kindly get in touch
with A. Aisner, 24 Thane street,
Dorchester.

     The Harmony Club theatre party
on Friday evening, March 16th, was
greatly enjoyed by all the members.
The performance at B. F. Keiths
Theatre was witnessed after which
the members filed into the St. Regis.
The Committee in charge consisted of
E. Beck, R. Schwartz, I. Stern, D.

     The first annual dance of the
Utopian Club, held at Cotick Bungalow,
Malden, was one of the most
successful events of the season, both
socially and financially. The Utopian
Club is a co-ed organization and is
progressing very rapidly. At the
close of meetings, which are held
every other week, refreshments are
served and dancing takes place. The
offifficers of this club are: S. Cohen,
president; Miss G. Hadler, vice-president;
Mr. W. Corven, secretary;
Miss M. Novack, treasurer;
Harry Olitzky, chairman of social
committee.

     Miss Esther Elovitz, well known
club director and organizer, was presented
with a beautiful loving cup
by the Daughters of Zion, Sunday.
Miss Elovitz is very popular and 
well liked in Revere, because of
her pleasing character and good
work. A Purim play and entertainment
under the direction of Miss
Elovitz, was given Sunday, at the
Hebrew Educational Center by the
Daughters of Zion, with over  250
people present. The play was well
characterized by Beatrice Shafer,
Sylvia Chansky, Mildred Abkowitz
and Edna Isenberg. Pretty vocal
selections were given by Dora Huswick,
Ruth Mikelson and Goldie
Cohen. Recitations were rendered
by Thelma Mathews, Ruth Mikelson,
Beatrice Coyne, Evelyn Finkelstein,
Mollie Sherman, Annie Katzen,
Fannie Wilker, Sarah Barett and
Charlotte Regalsky, Rebecca Edelstein.
The story of Purim was told
by Clara Charney. Fancy dancing
was given by Mildred Abkowitz,
violin selections, Harold Palter and
Misses Schwartz, piano selections,
Rebecca Edelstein, Beatrice Shafer
and Sarah Barett. Many Palestinian
songs were sung by the club.
The proceeds are to go towards
charitable purposes.

New Publication of Women's
     League, United Synagogue
     Realizing the need for suitable
prayers which the young Jewish
mother can teach her little ones as
soon as they begin to talk, the Women's
League of the United Synagogue
has published a booklet of
prayers for young children. These
consist of extracts from the Standard
Prayer Book with an English
adaptation in easy rhyme. The booklet
will appeal especially to those
who are interested in having Morning
and Evening Prayers and Grace
before and after meals in easy and 
convenient form.
     The Women's League has also 
published cards containing the blessings
over the Sabbath and Hanukkah
Lights which are also sold at a
very nominal price.

Famous Russian Baritone
     For his concert in Jordan Hall,
Thursday evening March 29th, Alexander
Akimoff, the Russian baritone
from the Petrograd Opera House,
will present a most attractive program
of arias and songs which will
include two Jewish numbers specially
dedicated to him.
     Mr. Akimoff's career in Petrograd
was suddenly broken off by the war
and revolution. He left Russia and
toured Europe, adding to his laurels
in each country which he visited.
The press recognized him as a
talented interpretative artist, for he
learned the folk songs of each land
he visited, and touched the hearts of
his audience.
