Bejamin Ovrys and His Art

   By MARIE TROMMER

   No one but a man deeply sensitive and possessing a soul responding to every cry of pain, no matter how distant, could reproduce the horrors of a pogrom without actually having witnessed one.  It was Benjamin Ovryn, the young Jewish artist, who thus succeeded in depicting a massacre in Kolomea, Poland, as though he himself had been there at the time and lived through all the horrors. 
   Ovryn was born in Kiev, South Russia, and received his first art training at the Kiev Art School, continuing his studies after his arrival in this country a few years ago, at the National Academy of Design.  He made his American debut last Spring at the Independent Artists' Exhibition, with a few landscapes of the Hudson River and rural scenes, ably portraying their ever-changing coloring and picturesqueness.
   Ovryn's two pogrom paintings were exhibited at the Independent Artists' Show of this Spring, which closed on Sunday, March 18th.  Both canvases are striking in their originality of conception and heartrending realism.  Two Jews just killed by the savage mob and a policeman watching indifferently the terrible massacre, is the theme of one of the paintings.  The other shows some mutilated flesh that was a body, possibly only a few minutes previous. 
   Yet it would be wrong to think that Ovryn's art is morbid - he is to versatile for that, as is best borne out by his paintings of trees and other cheerful and peaceful subjects, expressive compositions, strong in line and color, exhibited last Fall at the Salons of America, where he is also going to exhibit this Spring.
   Mr. Ovryn is now at work on a huge canvas entitled "The Symphony of Death, Anguish and Horror," a painting in which his artistic soul  will find full self-expression.   The young artist, however, is far from concentrating on himself along - he believes in helping other aspirants and directs, free of charge, the School of Painting of the Y.M. and W.H.A. in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, devoting his talent and time to young Jewish embryo artists who may not have the opportunity or means to study art elsewhere.

                                        Fitchburg "Y" Elects

   Dr. Samuel Fine was elected president of the Fitchburg Y.M.H.A. at a largely attended meeting of the association Sunday afternoon.  These officers were also elected: Vice-president Harry Miller, recording secretary, Philip Rome; financial secretary, David Borowsky; treasurer, Harry Nathanson; trustees, Lewis Rome, Jacob Borosky, E.E. Dunn, A.H. Glassman, H. Student Hymen Penan and Alter Chiate.
   During the meeting 25 new members were added to the membership and it is expected that many more names will be added before the installation of officers in about two weeks.
   Lewis Coldberg, president of the Associated Y.M.H.A's of New England, is expected to be present at the installation.

                                             Nachman Heller in New London

                                             RAINY DAYS
                                             By A. Zeisler
  Not to put it in a hedge,
    Not for a train attendant -
  But for the glorious privilege
   Of being independent. 
                           -Robert Burns.

 I hereby make a motion,
  To be seconded and carried,
It is a warning, a friendly advice,
  To people single or married.

Give the matter a serious thought,
  Reflect and improve your ways,
It is absolutely necessary
  To be provided for rainy days.

Your happiness I have in mind,
  Your interest to enhance,
Save up a little money,
  When you have the chance.

Do not always depend,
  On the cloud's silver lining,
But work and make hay,
  While the sun is shining.

While you have the opportunity,
  Be bright and wide awake,
To spend all you earned,
  Is decidedly a big mistake.

Spend a little for pleasure,
  For enjoyments clean and pure,
Give something for charity,
  To help the needy and the poor.

But always think of the future,
  And save up a certain amount,
The wisest thing to do,
  It is to open a savings account.

If you neglect to be careful,
  You will surely live to repent,
As poverty in the declining years,
  Is a sad and unhappy event.

Finally I would admonish you,
  To take care of your reputation,
Be honest in your dealings,
  Avoid an alluring temptation,
A good name is always a safeguard,
  In every case and situation.

           Cambridge Whist Party Great Success

  A whist party was given Wednesday afternoon, March 7th, by Mrs. PInkos at her home, 16 Highland avenue, Cambridge.  She was assisted by Mrs. M.L. King of Massachusetts avenue and Mrs. Charak of Magazine street.  Prizes were given to the women and a collation was served assisted by Mrs. Shoenfeld.  On the whole, the women spent a very enjoyable afternoon and aside from the social end of the party $120.00 was made which was given to the Cambridge Hebrew Women's Aid Society of which most of those who attended were members.
   The Cambridge Hebrew Women's Aid Society is very active in Cambridge and suburbs and has been very busy this Winter in particular since there has been so much sickness as well as lack of fuel and unemployment.

             Crawford Street Sisterhood

A whist and dance for the benefit of its war orphan fund will be given by Sisterhood Beth Hamedrash Hagodal in the vestry of the synagogue on the evening of March 19th.  Mrs. Rittenbers is chairman of the committee.
   At the last regular meeting of the Sisterhood on March 6th, plans were formulated for the Monster Bazaar which will be held May 28-29-30-31st, on the grounds and in the vestry of the  synagogue.  At the close of the meeting, Rabbi H.H. Rubenovitz gave an interesting talk on "Menace of the Polish Jew," which appeared in the World's Work."
